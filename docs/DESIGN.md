# Trust iOS design

## Direction

Trust uses a cool porcelain canvas, white elevated surfaces, deep navy text, clear blue actions, and teal positive states. Light tokens are paper `#F6F8FC`, canvas `#EEF2F8`, surface `#FFFFFF`, ink `#14233B`, muted `#617089`, accent `#245CE7`, and positive `#168879`; `TrustTheme.swift` is their implementation source. The sleepy-animal avatars supply the hand-drawn character; keep functional screens calm, spacious, and unmistakably native iOS. Carry this palette through light mode while keeping the established deep-navy dark mode. Keep the current app icon in place; app-icon redesign work is paused.

Use rounded system typography, clear hierarchy, 24 pt page gutters, a consistent 20 pt title inset below the safe area on non-map tabs, soft 18–24 pt corners, and controls at least 44 pt tall. Use illustration sparingly: the profile gallery and real profile pictures carry the animal art into the app. Avoid decorative symbols that could be mistaken for a person's real profile. Keep Stop, Pause, visibility, and other consent controls plain and clear.

## Brand and launch copy

The product name is **Trust**. Lead with the product category—location sharing for iPhone—and the concrete benefit of reviewing location checks made in Trust. Do not append a permanent slogan to the wordmark or repeat an emotional tagline across the website, screenshots, and listing. App Store name `Trust: Location Sharing` and subtitle `See who checked your location` are working proposals in `AppStore/LISTING-COPY.md`; repository changes do not update the live App Store Connect listing.

Prefer ordinary language about who can see what and when. Never imply that the person sharing approves each snapshot check, that Trust can reveal checks made in other apps, that a push notification is guaranteed, or that location is not stored before someone checks. Describe a snapshot as the latest available location, not as guaranteed-current GPS. The runtime labels Off / Sealed / Always / Look / View remain unchanged until the proposed replacement vocabulary is tested, implemented across the interface, and reviewed in all shipped languages.

## Screen intent

- **Sign in:** present a modest Trust wordmark and the current motif, then a recognizable “Sign in to Trust” heading. State that sharing stays off until a mode is chosen for each person. Sign in with Apple is primary; Terms, Privacy, and Support remain reachable.
- **Handle:** explain the public handle rules, show availability feedback, and keep submission disabled for invalid or taken handles. Offer an optional profile-picture shortcut and an explicit, default-off “Help people find me” choice. Continue sends discovery consent version 1 only when the person turns that choice on; changing a picture never enables discovery or changes sharing.
- **Phone:** use one clearly disclosed Send code action to request a one-time verification text. Show “Phone verification is required.” above the field and the one-time-text agreement, “Standard message and data rates may apply,” frequency, HELP, and STOP directly below the button. The keyboard submit action must not send a code. After sending, keep code entry and Verify visible without scrolling. Require a valid handle and server-verified phone before the main app, including internal builds; provide no Later/skip action. Already verified accounts do not request another text on a new device. Allow the first number and two distinct normalized corrections immediately, subject to all account, destination, and global caps plus destination pacing. After that, show a neutral gray disabled Send/Resend button with a live countdown and the exact local retry time. Keep Edit available and Verify usable during send cooldowns. Restore account-scoped retry timing and an unexpired challenge after relaunch without persisting the code. Keep Privacy and Terms at the bottom. The registered Twilio 2FA campaign and public `/sms` evidence must match this flow before it ships.
- **People:** keep the map and people list together. Empty states guide people to Sharing; connected people who are not sharing remain visible. Use the shared bottom navigation for People, Sharing, Activity, and You.
  - **Sharing:** keep Add someone prominent above incoming Requests, connected People, Sent requests, and secondary Home status. Add someone opens one native sheet with a compact close control, “Find your person” heading, a single reassurance that sharing stays off until chosen, and an autofocus “Handle or phone number” field. Search automatically after 350 ms using an exact handle or complete phone number with the device region or explicit country prefix; there is no Search button. Keep the sheet visually light: one compact bordered field, no duplicate guidance or decorative card shadows, and a simple result row with the opted-in picture and `@handle`. Put Send request in a clear full-width action. For a valid phone search with no eligible match, show a plain no-match state and “Share invite link.” This wording describes the action without claiming the number is not registered: phone discovery is opt-in. Tapping opens native sharing; only then is the legacy invite link created, and dismissing the share sheet does not mean it was sent. Do not offer an address book, invite-code entry, or an automatic Trust-sent text. Preserve the current TestFlight install-and-reopen path for a recipient without the app; do not invent a public store URL. Incoming requests have Accept and Decline; sent requests show Pending and Cancel. Accepted connections start with both share directions Off. Connected people keep one compact row with name on the left and Off / Sealed / Always on the right; Pause and Remove are secondary actions. Keep per-person “Let [person] know when I’m Home or Away” inside that person’s secondary actions menu, default off; its copy says it shares status, not location, and also permits a Home-arrival alert while a share mode is active. Off and Pause suppress status and alerts; Hidden is never shown; removal revokes the connection. Stack mode controls when width or text size requires it. Confirm Stop sharing and Remove in a centered alert with the person's name. Home / Away / Hidden remains secondary because it controls what others see. Adding someone never starts location sharing.
- **Activity:** show recorded events and receipts with their status and timing; keep the empty state calm and useful.
- **You:** focus on profile picture, a visible and copyable personal handle, an explicit “Help people find me” toggle, membership, My location, appearance, and account. Explain that discovery lets people with the complete phone number find the handle, and that the profile picture can appear in exact handle or phone matches. Discovery starts off for existing accounts. Turning it off prevents future phone matches and unconnected picture previews; an active connection still authorizes the existing picture view. Tap the avatar with its camera badge to open the shared picture picker. Keep Terms, Privacy, and Support links fixed at the bottom of the screen. My location opens a detail screen for device permission and Home place, while sharing status controls live in Sharing. Appearance is a compact System / Light / Dark preference saved on this device, with System as the default.
- **Profile pictures:** offer 11 sleepy-animal illustrated choices in a horizontal carousel, with no visible names; expose each choice's name to accessibility. Keep the selected picture large and centered in the preview. Put Photos, Camera, and Remove picture in a native menu attached to the preview avatar; do not add a separate photo section. Use a compact sheet that can expand when needed. Stage changes until Save and disable editing while a save is running. A picture appears in exact discovery matches only when the account has opted in; otherwise it remains visible to active connected people. Search previews are small inline thumbnails, never public URLs. Removing a picture falls back to initials. Preserve initials for missing or failed photo loads. The selected art direction is **hand-drawn doodle**: purposeful slightly uneven navy pen strokes, matte muted fills, subtle asymmetry, and one sleeping animal on a quiet background. The transparent art sits on a shared adaptive disc (`#FCFAF2` in light appearance, `#334055` in dark appearance) so the illustrations do not retain a white square in dark mode. Avoid redundant contours and fur hatching. Each subject must read clearly inside a 44 pt circular crop. No text, border, or glossy depth. Sleepiness is identity art and must never indicate whether someone is offline, safe, or sharing.

  The 11 selected sleepy-animal illustrations are the current preset catalog in `apps/trust-ios/Resources/Assets.xcassets`; it includes Siamese, Ragdoll, and British Shorthair cats. Keep legacy preset IDs readable for saved profiles even when the picker no longer offers them. New IDs must be accepted by the API before they are offered in the app. In the offline demo, preset edits last for the current session and photo actions are disabled with an explanation; the live app uses authenticated save behavior.
- **Plus:** use StoreKit's subscription products for price and period, preserve restore and policy links, and show purchase or restore errors clearly.

## Duo layout target

The closed Duo keeps the compact phone experience. When open, the map stays on the left and people/detail navigation stays on the right. The adaptive People layout and width-aware Map route retain one navigation stack across layout changes. Keep profile and sharing controls legible in both appearances and at larger text sizes; device-specific test evidence belongs in `FEATURE-E2E-PLAN.md`.

## Product language

Sharing is a per-person choice. Avoid implying that Trust guarantees delivery of push notifications or SMS beyond the verification flow. Describe map, presence, and activity only as implemented; distinguish a one-time snapshot from live sharing.

In customer-facing copy, keep Trust's activity scope explicit: it records checks and views made in Trust. A snapshot check is the viewer's action under a mode the other person already enabled; it is not a fresh approval request. Say “latest available location” when freshness is not guaranteed. Keep privacy, retention, age, pricing, and subscription claims tied to the current implementation and approved policy; do not change legal text as part of a visual-copy refresh.

For an Always share, label the point as the latest location available and show its age beside People rows, map pins, and selected-person details. After five minutes, add a clear “may be out of date” cue; this is presentation-only and must not increase location polling. If Always is on but an entitled viewer has no point, say that the location is unavailable. A viewer without Plus keeps the existing Plus access treatment when live points are withheld. A Sealed point remains labeled as a one-time snapshot with its date and time. In the map details, put the snapshot time or live-location age on a full-width wrapping line ahead of secondary presence. Bound the compact map height and allow vertical scrolling so selected-person actions remain reachable above the tab bar; the wide details panel scrolls independently. When the required age check is unavailable or an authenticated person declines age-range disclosure, offer Stop sharing with everyone only as an explicitly confirmed privacy action; show pending, confirmed, and unconfirmed outcomes while keeping the age gate closed.

Presence feedback belongs to the choice it acknowledges. When another authorized Home/Away/Hidden choice is accepted, retire the preceding presence notice while its response is pending; preserve unrelated notices and publish success only after acknowledgement.
