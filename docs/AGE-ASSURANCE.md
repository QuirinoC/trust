# Trust age assurance

Updated 2 October 2026. This is the current implementation record and open product gate. It is not legal advice or a claim of compliance in every jurisdiction.

## Product rule

Trust does not impose a blanket 13+ age gate just because other family-location apps do. Age checks and consent steps should be added only when Apple or an applicable law requires them. That is a product direction, not a legal conclusion. Trust's intended audience classification and any child-account policy remain unresolved and need counsel review before public release.

For the planned U.S.-only launch, FTC guidance says COPPA applies to child-directed online services collecting children's personal information and to general-audience services when they have actual knowledge that they are collecting personal information from a child under 13. The FTC says COPPA does not require a genuinely general-audience service to ask users' ages. This does not determine whether Trust is general-audience, mixed-audience, or child-directed; counsel must assess Trust's intended, likely, and actual audience and its product and marketing. FTC factors include subject matter, visual content, characters and activities, models, language, advertising, intended audience, and empirical audience evidence. Family-focused marketing, especially creative centered on children, is part of that analysis.

The distinction affects whether Trust's current under-13 outcome is suitable. FTC guidance describes mixed-audience services as a narrow subset of child-directed services: they may use a neutral age screen only if they collect no personal information before screening, and for under-13 users they must either avoid collecting personal information or obtain verifiable parental consent. Mixed-audience services may not block children from participating altogether. General-audience services may choose to block under-13 users, but they are not required by COPPA to ask age. Trust currently blocks account creation after Apple's age-range response indicates under 13 and has no parent-managed account or COPPA verifiable-consent flow. Counsel must determine the correct audience classification and whether this flow is acceptable before public launch; a 4+ App Store rating does not answer that legal question.

The FTC's 2025 COPPA amendments were published on 22 April 2025 and generally required covered operators to comply within one year (22 April 2026). They include separate verifiable parental consent for specified third-party disclosures, limits against indefinite retention, and expanded security/data-governance duties. If counsel determines COPPA applies, review the current system against the amended rule, including Trust's telephone numbers, account identifiers, precise location, optional photos, retention, deletion, vendors, and any analytics or advertising disclosures. Trust should keep child-directed ad SDKs and targeted advertising out of the launch design. Before expanding beyond the U.S., review each market's laws and Apple's regional age-assurance requirements, then ensure App Store territories match that review. See the [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), [FTC 2025 amendments](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data), and [Apple's age-assurance guidance](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance/).

## Current implementation

- On iOS 26.2 and later, Trust checks Apple's `isEligibleForAgeFeatures` before requesting an age range. On iOS 26.4 and later, it reads `requiredRegulatoryFeatures` only after Apple reports eligibility. This ordering follows [Apple's current Declared Age Range guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/declaredagerange/requesting-people-share-their-age-range-with-your-app) and was corrected after earlier TestFlight startup feedback.
- When Apple indicates a required age flow, Trust requests Apple's shared age range and uses it on-device. A returned range below the lowest applicable gate blocks setup. An authenticated person who declines can still explicitly confirm Stop sharing with everyone; success leaves account access restricted and does not grant age authorization. A declined response stays distinct from a below-gate result; missing or overlapping bounds remain indeterminate and restricted. If Apple's required service cannot complete, Trust fails closed for that flow instead of silently falling back to a global birth-date check.
- iOS 26.4 and later uses Apple's per-person regulatory features for significant app changes. iOS 26.2–26.3 uses Apple's eligibility and shared range to decide whether parental approval is needed for a significant change. The app uses the verified StoreKit `originalAppVersion` to avoid asking new installs to acknowledge a change they already installed with.
- iOS 26.0–26.1 does not provide the regional eligibility signal used here. Trust does not infer a universal age rule from that gap. A previously established under-minimum block remains restricted and directs the person to Support.
- Trust does not store a date of birth, age range, or derived age band on the server. The range is used transiently on-device; it is not included in ordinary account or location requests.
- Before loading an authenticated account, the app links its signed StoreKit App Transaction to that Trust account. This lets the server enforce Apple's later consent-revocation notification. The transaction is not proof of age or initial parental consent. If this link cannot be verified, account data and background location stay locked behind a transaction-specific retry; the app does not present it as an age-check failure. On revocation, the API blocks access while account deletion is retried durably; self-service deletion remains available.

## App Store age rating

On 30 September 2026, App Store Connect showed a calculated 4+ rating but had a manual 18+ override. The 18+ override was removed and the saved rating was verified after navigating away and returning: 4+ in 172 countries or regions, with Apple's regional equivalents, and a 4+ legacy rating for operating systems earlier than iOS 26. This follows the product direction against blanket age exclusion; it does not decide whether Trust is general-audience, mixed-audience, or child-directed, or settle COPPA duties. Counsel must still classify the audience and validate the intended territories. Apple's runtime age checks remain conditional on Apple's regional eligibility and regulatory signals.

## Launch gaps

- The current age flow does not provide a parent-managed child account or first-use parental-consent path. Do not market Trust as supporting child accounts until the audience classification and legal obligations are reviewed and any required flow is implemented. If counsel classifies Trust as mixed-audience or child-directed for COPPA, resolve the mismatch between the current under-13 block and required participation/consent handling before launch.
- Apple age-range and parental-approval scenarios have not been proven on a signed physical device. Diana submitted two reports on internal build 42, iPhone 16 Pro Max / iOS 26.6.2, on 1 October; “No me deja” and both screenshots show “We can’t complete the required check.” The owner then reported the exact Trust screen “Couldn’t verify this app” persisted on build 43. Astra identified this as the AppTransaction registration stage after sign-in and age authorization, but the precise local StoreKit vs server/transport cause is unconfirmed. On explicit Retry, source refreshes `AppTransaction` from Apple only if the shared result throws or is unverified; verified transactions retry server linking. Cancellation propagates, and API dispatch rechecks account and age-authorization generation. Sanitized logging distinguishes those stages. Builds 44 and 45 are **Testing** in `Trust Family Auto`; App Store Connect showed three invites for build 45 at 11:37 AM PDT on 2 October. Diana's physical retest of build 45 is ready. Keep account/location access fail-closed. Builds 43–45 skip phone verification for internal testing; never submit them for public review. Simulator fixtures do not exercise Apple's live age service.
- Local simulator coverage uses deterministic DEBUG and loopback fixtures. It covers failed Apple-token exchange and retry into onboarding, App Transaction link failure and recovery across retry/relaunch, and suppression of account, location, and push requests while age or privacy checks are blocked. The three proxy-backed scenarios passed on iPhone 17 Pro/iOS 26.5; the final age-gate UI suite passed 15/15, including authenticated decline Stop confirmation/pending states and unauthenticated exclusion. All seven isolated fault cases passed again after that privacy fix; see [3 October UX review](UX-REVIEW-2026-10-03.md) for result bundles and the DEBUG Stop-success fixture limit. The latest Swift package suite passes 97/97 and the focused AppTransaction-unavailable UI test passed. The CI-equivalent direct-API run passed its four supported cases and explicitly skipped eight cases requiring paired-device or fault-proxy fixtures. The proxy returns a controlled transaction response; these tests do not validate a real StoreKit-signed App Transaction with Apple's server verifier or call Apple's live age service. See [release status](STATUS.md) and [App Store review readiness](../apps/trust-ios/AppStore/REVIEW-READINESS.md) for current build and CI evidence.
